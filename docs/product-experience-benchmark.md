# Product experience benchmark — July 2026

## Scope

Focused review of current public entry journeys from Otovo, Enpal, Sunsave, EnergySage, SolarApp and Energy Saving Trust, plus the existing raportsolar.ro experience. The comparison covers interaction entry, question density, progressive disclosure, trust, result hierarchy and commercial framing.

## Patterns worth adopting

- Put the first meaningful choice close to the top. Otovo and EnergySage lead with location or property context instead of a long technical introduction.
- State the benefit before asking for data. Enpal and Sunsave frame the journey around savings, while Energy Saving Trust explains system size, cost and savings in plain language.
- Ask one primary question at a time and group only closely related inputs.
- Use visual, labelled selections for conceptual choices; reserve dropdowns for long lists or technical details.
- Confirm why an answer matters and expose assumptions close to the result.
- Lead results with one recommendation and a few supporting outcomes, then progressively reveal technical traceability.
- Preserve a clear next action after the result.

## Patterns deliberately not adopted

- Installer lead capture as the main result.
- Claims of guaranteed savings or artificially precise payback.
- Mandatory contact details before showing useful guidance.
- Equal-weight promotion of every calculator.
- Sales-led urgency, proprietary illustrations or copied layouts.

## RaportSolar differentiation

RaportSolar remains an **independent technical and financial second opinion for photovoltaic decisions and offers in Romania**. The offer-analysis product is the primary commercial proposition. Recommendation and location exploration prepare the customer to request and verify a suitable offer; they do not distribute the customer's data to installers.

## Applied principles

1. Compact task-page entry and natural Romanian terminology.
2. Reusable, accessible visual choice cards with visible icons, descriptions and structural selected states.
3. Mobile-first sticky actions and focused question content.
4. Progressive input summary without a premature recommendation.
5. Result hierarchy: recommended range, preferred option, production, investment, payback, confidence and battery conclusion.
6. Technical assumptions remain available after the customer-facing conclusion.
